0Executive summary (one-page conclusion)
MECHANISM VALIDATED · AGONISM PROTECTIVE

Amylin-receptor agonism is a clinically validated weight-loss axis; AMYR3 (CALCR+RAMP3) sits at its core, but "AMYR3 as a distinct drug target" remains an engineering thesis, not a validated biology

Every marketed or late-stage amylin-based obesity therapy acts through CALCR-containing receptors (AMY1/2/3, and for some molecules the bare calcitonin receptor) — i.e., all of them engage AMYR3 to a degree. Genetic, clinical-precedence and structural data triangulate on CALCR/RAMP3: a genome-wide BMI signal maps inside CALCR (L2G score 0.84), pramlintide/calcitonin approvals are recorded by OTP as gain-of-function = protective clinical evidence, and direct cryo-EM structures of the AMYR3–Gs complex bound to amylin, pramlintide and cagrilintide are public. The open questions are subtype specificity (does RAMP3-based signalling matter clinically, vs RAMP1/2?) and modality (injectable peptide = validated; oral peptide / oral small molecule = unproven).

One-line judgment: The CALCR/amylin-receptor axis is among the strongest-validated non-incretin obesity mechanisms in 2026 — but its commercial and scientific value no longer resides in "proving the target"; it resides in differentiation (RAMP-subtype/CTR selectivity, tolerability, dosing convenience), where the AMYR3-specific claim is still hypothesis-grade and crowded by ≥8 competitive programs.

AMYR3 is an extracellular-facing class-B (secretin-family) GPCR heterodimer. The clinically validated modality is injectable peptide agonism; the tractability question is whether AMYR3-relevant agonism can be delivered orally or with subtype selectivity.

◆FAERS / safety-signal layer

  • No FAERS records exist for CALCR or RAMP3 as primary targets (OTP safetyLiabilities empty) — expected: no approved drug lists CALCR as its primary target; amylin analogues act as agonists without an FDA "target X" designation. FAERS must be replaced by the class-level clinical safety record:
  • Pramlintide (approved 2005, T1D/T2D adjunct): nausea/vomiting/anorexia are the dominant AEs; boxed warning for severe hypoglycaemia when co-administered with insulin (mechanism: amylin delays gastric emptying + glucagon suppression — potentiated by prandial insulin). Relevant to obesity programmes only when combined with insulin/secretagogues.
  • Newer long-acting analogues: cagrilintide GI-AE discontinuation 1.3% (REDEFINE 1 sub-analysis, EASD 2025); petrelintide ≈ placebo-level GI profile (max-effective-dose arm: 0 vomiting, 0 GI discontinuations; AE discontinuation 4.8% vs 4.9% placebo; vomiting 3.0% vs 6.2% placebo) — GI burden appears titratable and class-differentiable.
  • Calcitonin-receptor tonic-activation watch-item: CTR mediates osteoclast inhibition; chronic full CTR agonism (DACRA-type molecules) could in theory alter bone turnover — historical salmon-calcitonin chronic-use data (long-term rodent osteosarcoma signal at high dose; human relevance judged low) keep this a regulatory/toxicology watch-item, not a development stopper. Eloralintide's AMY1R-over-CTR engineering is explicitly motivated by this concern (companion pharmacology report).
  • Fatigue signal reported at higher eloralintide doses (up to ~43–46% at 9 mg across escalation arms; secondary reports) — a non-GI AE worth tracking across the class.
  • Toxicology blind-spot honesty: long-term (≥2-yr) rodent carcinogenicity and chronic non-clinical tox for the newest analogues are not public; human Phase-1 AE rates do not substitute for GLP tox — the EP262 lesson applies (long-term tox can terminate a programme independently of mechanism validation).

5Five-step synthesis — turning OTP data into a judgment

◆Step 1 — Lock the pair; read the association score

CALCR×obesity = 0.34–0.42 (rank #114/9,702); RAMP3×obesity = 0.11 (rank #1,245). Interpretation: moderate-to-good for a non-Mendelian common-disease mechanism whose genetic anchor is a single (if reproducible) BMI/EHR-obesity GWAS locus inside CALCR. The score under-states biological validity because (a) the highest-weight evidence class for this target — human clinical agonism data — is only partially captured (ChEMBL lag) and (b) the RAMP3 half of the complex is inherently under-scored by a gene-centric platform.

◆Step 2 — Evidence-type weighting

Genetics (×2 modifier): partial credit — a robust common-variant locus at CALCR (multiple GWAS, L2G to CALCR) but no monogenic/rare-variant obesity signal; treat as a modest positive, not a full genetic endorsement. Clinical precedent: the decisive tier — pramlintide (approved) + ≥8 active clinical amylin programmes including an NDA under FDA review. Animal model: strong but non-OTP (diet-induced-obesity pharmacology literature: amylin/DACRA efficacy in rodents; KBP-042/BGM1812/DACRA+semaglutide studies). Expression: supportive but GTEx-incomplete for the key CNS nuclei (hindbrain AP/NTS, hypothalamus) and for RAMP3 (bulk blind spots; Tabula Sapiens shows strong endocrine-pancreas signal; single-cell/IHC literature needed).

◆Step 3 — Modality matching

  • Peptide agonist (weekly SC): validated — the entire clinical class.
  • Long-acting peptide (weekly→monthly): validated kinetics, now in Ph3.
  • Oral peptide (weekly, absorption enhancer): Ph1, unproven exposure — the key technical de-risking question for VRB-103.
  • Oral small-molecule agonist of class-B GPCR: frontier — ACCG-2671 Ph1; historically low success for agonism at peptide GPCRs by orthosteric small molecules.
  • Subtype-selectivity engineering (AMY1/3 vs CTR): structurally supported by PDB agonist series (7TYF/7TYH/7TZF/8F0K/8F2A/8F2B/9BTW) and now tested clinically (eloralintide AMY1-over-CTR; VRB-103 AMY1/3-over-CTR).

◆Step 4 — Direction check

Agonism = protective (GoF rows native in OTP; human dose-response across every analogue; direction settled beyond reasonable doubt). Direction error risk ≈ 0 — this is the single strongest pillar of the assessment.

◆Step 5 — Safety & traffic-light overlay

  • No OTP safety liabilities; no FAERS entries; clinical AE profile class-defined and titratable; modern analogues approach placebo-level GI discontinuations.
  • CTR-tonic agonism (bone) in DACRA-type molecules — watch-item; calcitonin historical chronic-rodent signal.
  • Fatigue signal at high-dose eloralintide; hypoglycaemia only in insulin contexts (pramlintide boxed warning analogue).
  • CALCR genetic constraint LOEUF 0.73 + heterogeneous Calcr-KO records (bone/Ca phenotypes; an allele record of complete-penetrance embryonic lethality) — relevant mainly to loss-of-function toxicology thinking, which is not the development direction (agonism).
  • Independent veto channel stays open: long-term GLP tox of the newest peptides is unpublished — the EP262 lesson applies regardless of mechanism strength.

Illustrative conclusion (deliberately non-quantitative): the relevant probabilities split by question. (i) "Will an amylin-receptor-agonist-based obesity drug reach the market?" — high: cagriSema is under FDA review on positive Phase-3 data with an approved-class precedent; a ≥60–70% chance that ≥1 amylin-based product (cagriSema first) is approved within ~3 years is a reasonable qualitative band. (ii) "Will a de novo AMYR3-selective or AMY1/3-selective programme entering Phase 1 today (e.g., oral VRB-103-type) succeed commercially?" — much lower, single-digit-to-low-teens territory: mechanism de-risked, but the programme must beat the efficacy bar set by incretins and differentiated incumbents on an unproven (oral) modality. The market has already demonstrated it will punish "10% weight loss with great tolerability" (petrelintide −30% share move).
Method B (ML, brief): OTP-family classifiers trained on historical successes would systematically under-rate this pair: features such as animal model/differential expression are absent or thin for RAMP3, and genetic weight is capped at common-variant level. Any automated readout should be custom-featured with clinical-pipeline status and subtype-expression evidence, otherwise it reproduces the "low score = weak target" fallacy for a mechanism with NDA-stage human validation.
7Limitations of this assessment (read first)
  • Score ≠ quality: CALCR/RAMP3 scores are evidence-abundance measures. The low-to-moderate numbers co-exist with NDA-stage human validation in the same mechanism — the score is not a verdict on the target.
  • Complex-granularity blind spot (structural): OTP scores genes, not the CALCR+RAMP3 heterodimer; AMYR3's identity is split across two under-linked gene records. RAMP3's low score is partly a platform-granularity artefact. This is an inherent scoring-architecture gap, not fixable by data refreshes.
  • RAMP3 GWAS silence (structural, verified): GWAS Catalog lists 19 RAMP3-locus associations but none for obesity itself (only a BMI-adjusted-waist hit at locus level where the causal gene is ambiguous) — treat as genuine absence of obesity GWAS support for RAMP3 specifically; the BMI signal lives at CALCR.

8Recommended operational path
  1. Position the question correctly before any go/no-go: "Is CALCR/amylin-receptor agonism valid for obesity?" is answered (yes, clinically). The live questions are: (a) does RAMP3-based (AMY3R) signalling add efficacy/safety value over pan-AMY or AMY1-preferring agonism; (b) can an oral or subtype-selective format differentiate in a −20%+ efficacy market. Answer (a) with AMYR3-selective tool molecules in DIO + hindbrain-lesion models and RAMP3-conditional-KO physiology, plus Pioszak-type subunit-equilibrium pharmacology.
  2. Decide modality risk budget: injectable peptide = minimal technical risk but commoditised; oral peptide/small molecule = differentiated but unproven — gate progression on Ph1 exposure/pharmacodynamics (VRB-103 end-2026; ACCG-2671) before Ph2 commitments.
  3. Set observation nodes (H2 2026 – 2027): cagriSema PDUFA (~Oct 2026); AZD6234 Ph2b at EASD 2026 (Sep 28–Oct 2); ZUPREME-2 topline (H2 2026); VRB-103 Ph1 data (end 2026); REDEFINE 11 (H1 2027); zenagamtide SC-obesity Ph3 first reads (2027).
  4. Toxicology diligence: request/obtain long-term GLP tox + carcinogenicity summaries for the analogue class; track CTR-bone turnover markers in any long-duration Ph2/3 (BMD substudies); monitor the eloralintide fatigue signal by dose/titration arm.
  5. Genetics follow-up (cheap, high value): fine-map the CALCR BMI locus (is the causal variant in CALCR coding/regulatory regions or a neighbouring gene?) and test AMYR3-subunit variant burden in large obesity exome cohorts — would convert "common-variant relevance" into causal clarity for AMYR3 specifically.
  6. Expression complement: single-nucleus/ISH mapping of CALCR+RAMP3 co-expression in human hypothalamus/hindbrain and pancreas to anchor AMYR3 (vs AMY1/AMY2) tissue biology beyond GTEx.
  7. Competitive positioning: assume the field will converge on tolerability + lean-mass preservation + convenience claims; an AMYR3-selective thesis must be supported by head-to-head or mechanistic differentiation data, not by target-name narrative.
